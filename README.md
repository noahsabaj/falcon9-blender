# Falcon 9 Block 5: a 1:1 procedural Blender model and short film

![The full Falcon 9 in rim light](docs/still_05_reveal.jpg)

A full-scale model of SpaceX's Falcon 9 Block 5 (1 Blender unit = 1 m, 70.0 m tall),
built entirely from Python in Blender 5.2. The repo also holds the code for a 65-second
short film, *FALCON 9*. It opens on the hardware in a dark studio, then follows a sunset
launch and the twilight "jellyfish" plume. Above the Earth come stage separation and the
fairing opening on a stack of Starlink satellites. The booster flips and lands on a
droneship at sunset.

Everything is generated by about 10,000 lines of Python: geometry, materials, rigging,
animation, effects, the edit, and the music and sound design. No meshes, textures, footage or
audio samples were downloaded.
The one outside input is the logo artwork: public-domain SVGs from Wikimedia Commons,
converted to polygons by a script (see [Sources](#sources-and-accuracy)).

Made with **Claude Opus 5.5** (Anthropic) in Claude Code.

**Watch the film:** see the [latest release](https://github.com/noahsabaj/falcon9-blender/releases/latest)
(`falcon9.mp4`, 1920x1080, 24 fps, 65 s; a version without music is attached too).

## Look at the model yourself

Download [`falcon9_block5.blend`](falcon9_block5.blend) (6 MB) and open it in
**Blender 5.2 or newer**. It has 101 objects, about 751k triangles and 28 procedural
materials, with no image textures.

- **Rig:** select the `F9_Rig` empty. Its custom properties `leg_deploy` and `gridfin_deploy`
  (0 to 1) swing out the four landing legs, with their telescoping pushers, and the four grid fins.
- **Cameras:** nine cameras frame the details: `Cam_Full`, `Cam_Base`, `Cam_Engines`,
  `Cam_Interstage`, `Cam_GridFin`, `Cam_Livery`, `Cam_Fairing`, `Cam_Landed` (legs and fins
  deployed) and `Cam_Cutaway`. `render.py` cuts a wedge out of the skins for the cutaway
  render, so `Cam_Cutaway` shows the internal structure only through that script.
- **Collections:** every subsystem has its own collection (`F9_Stage1`, `F9_Engines`,
  `F9_Legs`, `F9_GridFins`, `F9_Interstage`, `F9_MVac`, `F9_Stage2`, `F9_Fairing`,
  `F9_Livery`, `F9_Internals`), so you can hide or isolate it.
- **Axes:** the vehicle axis is world Z, with z = 0 at the plane of the Merlin nozzle exits.

### What is modelled

- **First stage:** 3.66 m tank barrel, aft skirt, base heat shield and octaweb, and nine
  Merlin 1D engines (one shared mesh, nine linked objects). Also the raceway and small
  fittings, four carbon-fibre landing legs with pushers, and four titanium grid fins with
  actuator housings. The legs and fins are rigged.
- **Interstage:** black composite shell, joint bolt pads, latch fairings, GN2 cold-gas
  thruster pods and grid-fin access ports.
- **Second stage:** tank and raceway, and the Merlin Vacuum engine with its niobium
  nozzle extension, turbine exhaust manifold, regeneratively cooled nozzle, chamber and
  turbomachinery. Also the PAF 1575 payload adapter.
- **Fairing:** two-piece composite fairing, 5.2 m x 13.15 m.
- **Livery:** the stacked SPACEX letters with swoosh, the Falcon 9 "F9" mark and the US flag.
- **Internals** (hidden by the skins; shown in cutaways): tank domes, LOX downcomer,
  octaweb thrust structure, COPVs and the second-stage thrust cone. These are plausible
  rather than exact.

## The film

| Time | Shot | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | `s01_nine` | Fade up on the nine Merlin engines from below; a light sweeps across the bells |
| 0:05 | `s02_lattice` | Macro glide across a titanium grid fin |
| 0:09 | `s03_livery` | Crane down the side past the SPACEX letters |
| 0:13 | `s04_interstage` | Orbit around the interstage: latches, cold-gas thrusters, grid fins |
| 0:17 | `s05_reveal` | Pull back to the full 70 m vehicle; title |
| 0:24 | `s06_ascent` | Hard cut: the climb at sunset, plume blazing |
| 0:28 | `s07_jellyfish` | Twilight: the plume blooms into a sunlit "jellyfish" high above |
| 0:34 | `s08_separation` | Above the Earth: MECO, stage separation, second-stage ignition |
| 0:41 | `s09_fairing` | The fairing halves fall away from the Starlink stack |
| 0:45 | `s10_flip` | The booster flips on cold-gas thrusters; grid fins deploy |
| 0:50 | `s11_landing` | Sunset at sea: landing burn, legs deploy, touchdown, steam |
| 0:58 | `s12_end` | The booster on the droneship at dusk; end titles |

The film pipeline lives in [`film/`](film):

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `cues.json` | The timeline: shot timings, sound events, intensity curve |
| `shots.py` | One builder per shot: environment, lights, camera and animation, applied to the model |
| `common.py`, `envs.py`, `fx.py` | Camera, keying and render helpers. Studio, physical sky, ocean, stars, procedural Earth and atmosphere. Exhaust plumes, vacuum plume, smoke, steam and cold-gas effects |
| `droneship.py` | A generic autonomous droneship (Marmac 300-class barge, blast walls, floodlights) |
| `payload.py` | A stack of 22 flat-panel Starlink-style satellites that fits inside the fairing |
| `make_shot.py`, `render_shot.py`, `render_all.py` | Build a shot file from the model, then render it (resumable) |
| `score.py` | The score and sound design, synthesized in numpy and scipy (additive, wavetable and filtered-noise synthesis, synthetic reverb) |
| `edit.py` | Assembles frames, titles, fades and audio into the MP4 |

## Rebuild everything

Requirements: Blender 5.2, Python 3.10+ with `numpy`, `scipy` and `Pillow`
(`pip install -r requirements.txt`), and `ffmpeg` on `PATH`. Cycles on an NVIDIA GPU with OptiX is strongly recommended. The titles use
Windows' Bahnschrift font; pass any TTF with `--font`.

```bash
# the model
blender --background --factory-startup --python build.py -- --out falcon9_block5.blend
blender --background falcon9_block5.blend --python render.py -- --outdir renders

# the film (set BLENDER=/path/to/blender if it is not at the default Windows location)
python film/render_all.py                               # build + render all 12 shots
python film/score.py --cues film/cues.json --outdir film/audio
python film/edit.py                                     # -> film/out/falcon9.mp4
python film/edit.py --no-music                          # -> film/out/falcon9_sfx_only.mp4
```

The full film is 1,560 frames at 1920x1080. On one RTX 4060 Ti the frames take about 80 minutes
to render (about 3 s each), plus a few minutes to build the shot scenes. `render_all.py` skips
frames that already exist, so it can be stopped and restarted.
`film/edit.py --draft` cuts a quick low-resolution version from preview renders
(`blender --background film/shots/<id>.blend --python film/render_shot.py -- --preview`).

## Sources and accuracy

- **Overall dimensions:** SpaceX's *Falcon User's Guide*: the 70 m stack, the 3.66 m core
  and the 5.2 m x 13.2 m fairing and its outline. The master dimensions are in [`f9/dims.py`](f9/dims.py).
- **Detail placement** (livery, leg planform, interstage details): measured from AXM's 1:100
  Falcon 9 Block 5 (CRS-18) paper-model sheets, used only as a measurement reference.
  None of AXM's artwork is included here.
- **Interstage and second-stage proportions:** launch photographs.
- **Logos:** public-domain SVGs on Wikimedia Commons,
  [SpaceX_logo_black.svg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SpaceX_logo_black.svg) and
  [Falcon_9_logo.svg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Falcon_9_logo.svg).
  [`tools/svg_to_livery.py`](tools/svg_to_livery.py) converts them to polygons in
  [`f9/data/livery_pd.json`](f9/data/livery_pd.json). The SVG files themselves are not included.
- **Film physics** is compressed for time, though it follows the real sequence and
  geometry. The vacuum plume, cold-gas jets, landing-burn deck flame and steam are
  artistic approximations. The droneship and satellites are generic look-alikes, not
  copies of specific vessels or spacecraft.
